giffgaff · Head of Member Experience · 2009–2015

99% self-service, and members who sold it for us

The world’s first 100% online mobile network, with no shops and no call centre. I built the member journey, the community and the referral scheme that made that work.

The situation

Launch, 2009. Every member had to get started, top up and fix problems on their own, at a time when mobile networks scored an NPS of around 20 and few people trusted buying a phone contract online.

The decision

Tell members when a cheaper plan suited them better, even though it cost revenue. “I’d rather have 80% of something than 100% of nothing.” Members trusted a network that tried to save them money, and they told their friends.

What stayed with people

The community. Members answered members, in public, good and bad. Automation carried the journey; people carried the judgement.

My role

All-digital mobile network · Interim Global CMO · 2025–26

Choosing a platform partner, with prototypes as proof

Eleven vendors, four countries, one contract. The gaps between what was demonstrated and what was contracted were caught before anyone signed.

The situation

A new mobile network across four countries, run without call-centre agents. The platform behind it is a long, expensive commitment, and what a vendor shows in a sales demo doesn’t always make it into the contract.

The decision

Score every vendor 60% technical, 40% commercial, and treat the sales demo as a promise the contract has to keep.

What AI made possible

More than 20 working conversations became one list of 404 requirements, and the vendor’s draft contract was checked against every one of them. That’s a job for a team of analysts, done without one. It also built the prototypes that made the requirements concrete, market by market.

What stayed with people

Every review comment was mine. Technical calls went to the technical lead, and commercial calls to procurement. AI did the comparing; people did the deciding.

My role

Interim Global CMO, across four countries. Co-wrote the request for proposals and led the review. Still in development, not launched.

Premier League football club · Head of Customer Service · 2017–20

Fewer queries, calmer fans, no extra seasonal staff

Three siloed teams became one, so the club could handle its busiest moments without temporary staff and answer anxious fans first.

The situation

Retail, ticketing and supporter services each ran as a separate team, each with its own peak: kit launches, membership renewals, Christmas. Each hired temporary staff for its rush. Fans sent the same question several times and were bounced between teams, and a worried email about this weekend’s match waited behind one about a game weeks away.

The decision

Merge three teams into one, so one team’s peak is covered by another’s quiet month, and put the next match first, because that’s when fans get anxious.

What stayed with people

A person still answers the anxious fan; the rule just makes sure they’re first. Advance notices and the help centre took the questions that didn’t need a person.

My role

Consultant on customer experience ahead of the new stadium opening, including a spell as interim Head of Customer Service. I audited every contact point, mapped the matchday journey and led the restructure.

Travel eSIM business · Requirements, journey design and automation

A travel eSIM service, mapped and working in six weeks

Around 400 requirements captured from meeting recordings, a six-flow customer journey, and a working service, ready to hand to a new development partner intact.

The situation

A new travel eSIM business wanted a service that ran on self-service, from a traveller’s first visit to after their trip. It needed the requirements agreed and a working service quickly, ahead of a wider public launch.

The decision

Capture every requirement traceably from day one, so the business could change development partner without starting again. It did, and the requirements and designs went with it.

What AI made possible

Meeting recordings became requirements tables, each line traceable to where it was agreed. The platform’s technical documentation was read for what customers could do for themselves. Six weeks, with no business analyst team.

What stayed with people

The team checked and agreed every requirement. Which of the 18 automation opportunities to build, and in what order, was a business decision, not a technical one.

My role

Requirements, journey design and the automation plan, working alongside the development partner. Not all of the self-service was built by the original partner; the business later moved to a new one.

Global sports and entertainment merchandise company · Brief, design and delivery · 2022–23

50+ online stores, one view of every order

Support agents went from hunting through dozens of shops to seeing a customer’s latest order the moment they open the ticket. Still running three years on.

The situation

Online shops for sports teams and entertainment brands: more than 50 stores worldwide, and a single brand could have 7, split by country and currency. The help desk’s standard connection linked to one store. Before agents could answer “where is my order?”, the most common question, they had to work out which shop the customer had bought from.

The decision

One connector, hosted in the client’s own environment, instead of 208 store-by-store rules nobody could maintain. It finds the latest order from the customer’s email address, across every store for that brand.

What stayed with people

What agents see first, and who is allowed to cancel or refund, were decisions for the client’s team. Admins can switch those actions on or off.

My role

Wrote the brief, designed the order panel and managed the developer through build and launch, testing it with the agents who’d use it every day. Done without AI; today the brief, the prototype and the checking of the build against the brief would take days, not weeks.
