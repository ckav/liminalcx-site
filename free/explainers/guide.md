4. **Plain English, no jargon.** If you have to use a technical word, explain it straight away.
5. **Be honest about safety.** In the "Staying safe" words, be practical, not scary.

## The words

### Files your AI can read
1. **README:** a short note at the top of a folder that says what's in it, which files are the latest and what happens next. Your AI reads one page instead of opening every file.
2. **Markdown (.md):** plain text with a few symbols for headings, bold and lists (`#`, `**`, `-`). Any AI tool, and any computer, can read it. It's the format AI tools like best.
3. **Second brain:** Tiago Forte's idea of keeping what you know in a trusted, organised place outside your head. His PARA method sorts it into Projects, Areas, Resources and Archive.

### Teaching your AI how you work
4. **Context:** everything the AI can "hold in mind" in one conversation: your messages, files and instructions. It has a limit. Long chats get forgetful.
5. **Memory:** things the AI remembers about you between conversations. Names differ between tools.
6. **Projects:** a space that keeps related chats, files and instructions together. Names differ between tools.
7. **Skills:** a saved set of instructions for one job, which the AI picks up when that job comes up. Started at Anthropic and now an open standard that many tools use.

### Connecting your AI to other tools
8. **Connectors:** ready-made links you switch on so your AI can see another app, such as your email, calendar or files. Names differ between tools.
9. **API:** the way two pieces of software talk to each other. Each app has its own, built for programmers.
10. **MCP (Model Context Protocol):** one standard plug that lets any AI tool connect to other apps. Often what's behind a connector. Its makers compare it to USB-C.

### AI that does things
11. **Agents:** AI that doesn't just answer but takes steps for you, such as reading, deciding, then doing, within the access you give it.
12. **Scheduled tasks:** a job your AI runs on its own at a set time, such as a 6am inbox summary. Names differ between tools.

### Staying safe
13. **Bad actors:** people trying to misuse AI, or misuse you through it. Cover:
    - hidden instructions in emails, web pages or documents that try to make the AI do something you didn't ask (called "prompt injection")
    - skills and connectors from sources you don't know
    - very convincing scam emails, messages and even cloned voices
    - pasting confidential information into tools your company hasn't approved
14. **Making things up:** AI sometimes states things confidently that aren't true. People call this "hallucination". How to spot it, and how to ask for sources.
